# Coaching Technique Library

Use these to decide *how* to say something, after the rubric and anti-pattern scan have told you
*what* needs to change. Default sequence for a live review conversation: **OARS opening → GROW
framing → Socratic questions on the highest-priority gap → SBI if direct feedback is needed →
close on a Will-stage commitment.** Skip stages the person doesn't need — e.g. if they explicitly
ask for a straight rewrite, don't force a Socratic back-and-forth first.

## OARS (open questions, affirmations, reflective listening, summarizing)
From motivational interviewing — draws out the person's own motivation instead of imposing
feedback, which is what keeps a review from triggering defensiveness.
- **Open questions**: "What would someone observe you doing differently if you hit this goal?"
- **Affirmations**: genuine, specific — "You've clearly scoped this to something you actually
  control," not a generic "good job."
- **Reflective listening**: paraphrase their intent back before critiquing — "So the real goal
  is owning the migration end-to-end, not just 'helping with' it — is that right?" People often
  self-correct on hearing their own logic restated.
- **Summarizing**: recap the whole goal back before proposing edits, so they know they were
  heard before anything changes.

## GROW (Goal → Reality → Options → Will)
Sequences reflection before solutioning — prevents jumping straight to "here's the fix."
- **G**: sharpen the goal statement itself — what do they actually want to be true?
- **R**: probe what's true right now that makes this hard (context, constraints, history).
- **O**: have *them* generate 2–3 ways to state or achieve it better before you offer your own.
- **W**: lock a revised version plus one committed next step (what they'll confirm, by when).

## Socratic questioning
Believes the fix is already in the person's head — a well-aimed question surfaces it faster and
with more buy-in than a correction does.
- Instead of "This is too vague" → "What would someone observe you doing differently if you'd
  hit this goal?"
- Instead of "You need a metric" → "How will you know, in a number, that this worked?"
- Instead of "This depends on Team X" → "What happens to this goal if Team X's timeline slips?"

## SBI (Situation – Behavior – Impact)
Use when a direct statement is actually needed (the person asked for it, or a Socratic pass
already happened and the gap is still there). Factual and non-accusatory — describes the pattern,
not the person.
- **Situation**: "In this goal,"
- **Behavior**: "you've written 'improve system reliability' with no metric,"
- **Impact**: "which makes it hard for you or your manager to agree at review time whether it
  was hit."

## Radical candor (care personally, challenge directly) — not a sandwich
State the gap plainly, paired with visible investment in the person's outcome — don't dilute it
by bookending with throwaway praise, which reads as manipulative and lets the real feedback get
discounted.
- Lead with genuine care: "I want this goal to actually hold up at review time,"
- then be direct: "right now there's no number in it, so it can't be scored,"
- then move straight into a collaborative question: "what's the metric you'd actually be judged
  on here?"

## Behavioral coaching (shaping + milestones)
For goals with no interim checkpoints — breaks an abstract, all-or-nothing goal into observable
steps.
- "What would the first checkpoint look like in six weeks?"
- Offer to co-draft a lightweight commitment: metric + deadline + check-in date.

## Sequencing cheat sheet

| Situation | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Person just pasted a goal, no context yet | OARS open question — understand intent before critiquing |
| Goal is vague/unmeasurable | Socratic question aimed at the specific gap, not a flaw list |
| Person explicitly asked "what's wrong with this" | SBI — they've asked for directness, give it |
| Person seems to already sense the goal is weak | Affirm the self-awareness, then GROW's Options stage — let them generate the fix |
| Goal has no milestones/check-ins | Behavioral coaching — ask about the first checkpoint |
| Review is done, goal is fixed | Close on GROW's Will stage — one concrete next step, not just the final sentence |

**Restraint is the tell.** A skilled coach asks one well-chosen question and waits for the answer
before adding more. Listing every flaw from the rubric in one pass is what makes a review feel
like a checklist bot instead of a coach — resist it even when you can see five things wrong.
