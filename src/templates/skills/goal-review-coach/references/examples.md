# Worked Examples

Two full reviews (showing the complete output template in practice), followed by compact
poor → average → excellent progressions across functions.

---

## Full example 1 — Engineering IC

**Draft goal:** "Help improve backend performance this year."

### Executive Summary
This goal names a real area (backend performance) but gives no metric, no scope, and no
deadline — as written it can't be scored at year-end by anyone, including the author. Biggest
lever: replace "help improve" with a specific, owned, measurable target.

### Overall Score: 1.5 / 5.0 — Needs Rework
Confidence: Medium — role and system context inferred from "backend," not confirmed with the
person.

### Dimension Scores (selected)
| Dimension | Score | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity & Specificity | 1/5 | "Help improve" — weasel verb, no scope |
| Outcome Orientation | 2/5 | Points at an outcome area but not a stated change |
| Measurability | 1/5 | No metric anywhere |
| Time-Bound | 2/5 | "This year" is a horizon, not a deadline; too long for no interim checkpoints |
| Ownership | 2/5 | "Help" implies shared/secondary ownership, not primary |

### Recommended Rewrite
> "Reduce p95 API latency on the checkout service from [current — confirm] to under 300ms by
> end of Q3, through query optimization and caching on the top 3 slowest endpoints."

### Next Coaching Step
Confirm current p95 latency and which service is highest-priority before locking the target.

## Full example 2 — Manager / Leadership

**Draft goal:** "Support my team's growth and make sure we hit our deliverables."

### Executive Summary
Two goals stitched into one ("goal soup"), both stated as activities ("support," "make sure")
with no metric for either. Split it: a team-growth goal and a delivery goal, each with its own
success measure.

### Overall Score: 1.8 / 5.0 — Needs Rework
Confidence: Low — no visibility into team size, current attrition/promo rate, or which
deliverables are meant.

### Weaknesses
- Goal soup: "team's growth" and "hit deliverables" are unrelated objectives.
- Both halves are activity-framed ("support," "make sure") with no outcome stated.
- No named metric for growth (promotions? retention? skill growth?) or for delivery (on-time %? scope?).

### Recommended Rewrite
> Split into two:
> 1. "Grow 2 of my 6 reports into the next level (promotion-ready) by year-end, via monthly
>    growth check-ins and stretch project assignments."
> 2. "Deliver [named roadmap items] on time, with no more than 1 slipped milestone across the
>    team this half, tracked via [system]."

### Next Coaching Step
Ask which specific deliverables and how many direct reports are in scope before finalizing
either half.

## Compact progressions by function

**Product**
- Poor: "Work on improving the onboarding experience."
- Average: "Redesign onboarding to reduce drop-off."
- Excellent: "Increase Day-1 activation rate from 38% to 50% by Q3 via a redesigned onboarding
  flow, measured in the activation funnel dashboard."

**Sales**
- Poor: "Help grow the pipeline."
- Average: "Generate more qualified leads in my territory."
- Excellent: "Grow qualified pipeline in the West territory from $2M to $3.2M by end of Q4,
  through 15 net-new outbound sequences per month."

**Marketing**
- Poor: "Support brand awareness initiatives."
- Average: "Run campaigns to increase brand awareness."
- Excellent: "Increase aided brand awareness from 22% to 30% (per quarterly brand survey) by
  Q4, via 3 integrated campaigns targeting the mid-market segment."

**Operations**
- Poor: "Improve process efficiency."
- Average: "Streamline the fulfillment process."
- Excellent: "Cut average order-fulfillment time from 3.2 days to 1.5 days by Q2, by automating
  the manual inventory-check step (owned end-to-end, no cross-team dependency)."

**Individual Contributor (general)**
- Poor: "Get better at my role."
- Average: "Take on more responsibility on the team."
- Excellent: "Own the migration of Service X to the new infra stack end-to-end, cutting
  monthly hosting cost by 20%, completed by end of Q3."

**Manager / Leadership (general)**
- Poor: "Be a better leader for my team."
- Average: "Improve team engagement and performance."
- Excellent: "Raise team eNPS from 24 to 40 (per Q3 pulse survey) by running biweekly 1:1s with
  documented growth plans for all 6 reports, starting this month."

### Pattern across all of them
Poor → average moves from a weasel verb to a slightly more specific verb, but still no metric.
Average → excellent is always the same move: **add the number** (baseline + target), **the
deadline**, and **make the ownership and mechanism explicit**. That one move — not longer
sentences — is what separates a workable goal from an excellent one.
